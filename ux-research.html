About Research

I approach each project by examining the relationship between people, interfaces, and the context in which products are used. I enjoy challenging assumptions, identifying underlying issues, and making sense of complex findings.


My work brings curiosity, critical thinking, and a structured approach to complex problems, with the aim of making digital products more understandable, inclusive, and purposeful.

Research Process

I begin with a question, explore the problem from different angles, and gradually turn complexity into clarity. My process is structured but flexible, allowing new discoveries to shape the direction of the work.

This UX audit provides a diagnostic analysis of the PayPal mobile application, specifically examining the structural integrity of its navigation and the precision of its information architecture. Unlike broader research, this evaluation identifies the exact "functional gaps" where current design choices—such as redundant menu links and clustered data visualizations—obstruct the workflow. The audit pinpoints where "semantic friction" in microcopy leads to user hesitation and a measurable "learning penalty". The findings transition from identifying individual friction points to proposing a systemic redesign aimed at restoring technical trust and visual order. The core objective is to replace the current "underguided" experience with a better performing interface that aligns with professional banking mental models. Through these targeted refinements in layout density and terminology, the audit establishes a framework for an application that prioritizes data auditability, operational speed, and an uncompromising sense of security.
